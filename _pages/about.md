<p class="intro intro--lead"><strong>I am interested in how field measurements can become <span class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">reliable, uncertainty-aware evidence</span> for civil infrastructure.</strong></p>

<div class="home-narrative">
  <p>Over seven years, I have developed, installed, and operated sensing systems in real service: monitoring four in-service bridges with our laboratory's wireless sensor node <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">Structural Health Monitoring</strong> <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">Wireless Sensing</strong>, improving freight-vehicle weighing at UDNS <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">Weigh-in-Motion</strong>, evaluating bridge capacity in the field <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">Bridge Load Testing</strong>, and recovering failed sensor channels (<strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">Machine Learning</strong>).</p>
  <p>Through this work, I have become most interested in what comes after the measurement: using field data to <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">evaluate</strong> the condition of real assets, <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">forecast</strong> how it will change over their lifecycle, and <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">support decisions</strong> about them. To do this rigorously, I want to build a foundation in <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">probabilistic modeling</strong>, <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">inverse problems</strong>, and <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">uncertainty quantification</strong>, and to learn how <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">physics-informed machine learning</strong> and <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">structural reliability</strong> can support these decisions.</p>
</div>
