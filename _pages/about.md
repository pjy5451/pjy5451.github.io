<p class="intro intro--lead"><strong>I am interested in how field measurements can become <span class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">reliable, uncertainty-aware evidence</span> for civil infrastructure.</strong></p>

<div class="home-narrative">
  <p>Over seven years, I have taken sensing systems into real service: monitoring four in-service bridges with our laboratory's wireless sensor node (<strong>Structural Health Monitoring</strong>) (<strong>Wireless Sensing</strong>), improving freight-vehicle weighing at UDNS (<strong>Weigh-in-Motion</strong>), evaluating bridge capacity in the field (<strong>Bridge Load Testing</strong>), and recovering failed sensor channels (<strong>Machine Learning</strong>).</p>
  <p>Along the way, my ability to <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">collect data</strong> grew faster than my ability to <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">interpret it</strong>. I now want to build a foundation in <strong>probabilistic modeling</strong>, <strong>inverse problems</strong>, and <strong>uncertainty quantification</strong>, and to learn how <strong>physics-informed machine learning</strong> and <strong>structural reliability</strong> can turn monitoring data into <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">maintenance decisions</strong>.</p>
</div>
