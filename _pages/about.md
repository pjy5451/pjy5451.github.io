<p class="intro intro--lead"><strong>From field measurements to <span class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">principled inference</span> for infrastructure.</strong></p>

<div class="home-narrative">
  <p>I am a researcher at UDNS in Korea, working on weigh-in-motion and on-board load monitoring for freight vehicles. During my M.S. at Chung-Ang University, I worked on developing JANET, our laboratory's wireless sensor node, and deployed it on <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">four in-service bridges</strong> in Seoul, recording at 1 kHz for over a year. Across 10+ field tests on bridges, roads, vehicles, and precast structures, I have worked on sensing hardware, installation, data pipelines, and signal processing.</p>
  <p>Along the way, I became increasingly interested in <strong class="text-highlight text-highlight--system">interpreting field measurements through more rigorous frameworks</strong>. When estimating axle loads from paired pavement and on-board signals, or separating temperature effects from structural change in bridge frequencies, I relied more on engineering intuition than on principled formulation. I now want to build a foundation in structural dynamics, probabilistic modeling, and inverse problems, so that field measurements can support conclusions with quantified uncertainty.</p>
</div>
